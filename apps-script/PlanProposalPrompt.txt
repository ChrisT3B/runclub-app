## How to plan

1. Fix the goal first. If the brief gives a view on the goal, use it. Otherwise say whether it looks achievable, aggressive or needs discussion, using the baseline and the paces above. Do not change the goal.
2. Place anchor sessions first: the long run and one or two quality sessions (tempo or interval). Work forward from week 1 to race day. Let the phases fall out of the progression, unless the brief specifies the phase structure. Phases are Base, Build, Peak, Taper, Race and Recovery.
3. Default plan lengths if the window allows: 5k or 10k 8 to 12 weeks, half marathon 12 to 16, marathon 16 to 20. If the window is shorter, say so.
4. Weekly volume: use the runner's current volume as the starting point. Progress gradually. Follow the load strategy in the plan settings (for example "3:1 step" means 3 weeks up, then 1 down week; "Linear" means no down weeks). A down week is lower than the week before by the down-week cut in the plan settings (0.25 means 25 percent less). Weeks disrupted by holiday or travel count as down weeks. Taper the final two weeks for a half marathon or longer.
5. Respect the runner's firm limits: days they can run, fixed rest days, time available per session on weekdays and weekends, work and other commitments, holiday and travel dates.
6. The runner's current weekly routine is a starting point, not a rule. You may move sessions, change rest days or add runs where it helps. Say why in the rationale.
7. Do not put an interval session and a tempo session on consecutive days unless the brief allows it. Do not put a hard session on a day marked as high stress or a fixture.
8. Introduce tempo and intervals gradually for a runner with little speed-work history.
9. Do not state paces in sessions. Paces are looked up from the sheet. A Tempo or Interval day string is the main block only: the sheet adds the warm-up and cool-down given in the plan settings (easy running before and after) to the weekly total and tells the runner. Do not write them in the day string and do not count them in "target_total_km".
10. Strength or gym days are sessions too. Write them as Strength with a duration.
11. If the brief specifies how to apply the paces, follow it. If it leaves it to you, judge how far to trust the paces from how recent the baseline race is, its distance compared with the goal distance, any conditions given, and the runner's speed-work history. Refer only to the paces given above; do not invent new pace numbers.
12. Follow every section the coach specified. If you think something the coach specified should change, you may depart from it only with a clear reason, and you must list it under "departures".
13. For every section marked "AI to choose", make the choice, using the default given unless the runner's answers suggest otherwise, and explain it under "choices".
14. "Training adjustments from health" and "Firm rules" are never departed from. If one cannot be met, say so under "departures" and plan as close to it as you can.
15. If the primary metric is Time, write Easy, Steady and Long runs as durations (for example Easy 40min, Long 95min) and give "target_total_km" as total minutes. If it is Distance or Pace, write them as distances. Do not convert between time and distance yourself; the coach's sheet does that from the paces.

## Session format (strict)

Each day is one text string. Use only these forms:

- Rest
- Easy 8km, Steady 10km, Long 16km, Race 21.1km (use "mi" instead of "km" for miles)
- Easy 40min, Long 95min (only when the primary metric is Time)
- Tempo 3x10min, Tempo 25min (the main block only; the warm-up and cool-down are added by the sheet)
- Interval 6x400m, Interval 5x1000m
- Cross 45min, Strength 60min

Allowed words at the start of a string: Easy, Steady, Tempo, Interval, Long, Race, Cross, Strength, Rest. No other words, no pace, no notes inside a day string.

## Explaining the plan (required)

The coach needs to see quickly how the plan relates to the brief:

1. Departures: anything you did differently from what the coach specified, and why. Empty if none.
2. Choices: for each section marked "AI to choose", what you chose and why.
3. How the plan follows the brief: how it carries out what the coach specified.
4. The goal view and the phase structure.
5. Answers to the coach's questions, one for each question in the brief.
6. Assumptions you made.

For every week, say what you chose and which of the runner's answers or brief sections drove it. Name them (for example "current weekly volume", "time available weekday", "holiday dates", "Firm rules"). State trade-offs and anything you were unsure about. Be plain and short. Do not repeat the sessions back.
